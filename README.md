1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
This project presents the design and development of a three-planet planetary gear system using SolidWorks.
The project was undertaken to understand the design principles, geometric constraints, kinematic relationships, CAD modelling techniques, assembly methodology, and motion behaviour of planetary gear systems.
The system consists of a central Sun Gear, three Planet Gears, an Internal Ring Gear, and a Planet Carrier. The gear tooth counts and overall geometry were determined using planetary gear relationships before developing the individual components in SolidWorks.
The completed components were assembled into a functional planetary gear mechanism and subjected to a basic Motion Study to investigate its kinematic behaviour.

3. SYSTEM CONFIGURATION
The planetary gear train consists of the following major components:
3.1 Sun Gear
The Sun Gear is positioned at the centre of the planetary system and meshes externally with all three Planet Gears.
3.2 Planet Gears
Three identical Planet Gears are arranged around the Sun Gear. The planets are equally spaced at 120° intervals to maintain symmetrical loading and positioning.
3.3 Ring Gear
The Ring Gear is an internal gear surrounding the Planet Gears. Each Planet Gear simultaneously meshes with the Sun Gear and the internal Ring Gear.
3.4 Planet Carrier
The Planet Carrier supports the Planet Gears and maintains their relative positions around the Sun Gear.
3.5 Planet Pins
Planet Pins are cylindrical features attached to the Planet Carrier. The Planet Gears rotate about these pins through their central bores.

5. PLANETARY GEAR THEORY
For a simple planetary gear system, the fundamental tooth-count relationship between the Sun, Planet, and Ring gears is:
Nᵣ = Nₛ + 2Nₚ
where:
•	Nₛ = Number of teeth on the Sun Gear
•	Nᵣ = Number of teeth on the Ring Gear
•	Nₚ = Number of teeth on each Planet Gear
This relationship ensures that the pitch geometry of the three gear types is compatible.
The kinematic behaviour of the planetary system can be described using the Willis equation:
(ωₛ − ω𝑐) / (ωᵣ − ω𝑐) = −Nᵣ / Nₛ
where:
•	ωₛ = Angular velocity of Sun Gear
•	ωᵣ = Angular velocity of Ring Gear
•	ω𝑐 = Angular velocity of Planet Carrier
The appropriate input, output, and fixed members determine the resulting gear ratio and direction of rotation.

The design process included:
i.	Selection of module.
ii.	Selection of pressure angle.
iii.	Selection of tooth counts.
iv.	Calculation of pitch diameters.
v.	Calculation of base circle diameters.
vi.	Calculation of addendum and dedendum.
vii.	Development of the involute tooth profile.
viii.	Determination of tooth thickness.
ix.	Creation of the final gear profile in SolidWorks.
x.	Verification of the mating geometry between gears.
The resulting gear profiles were incorporated into the Sun, Planet, and Ring Gear models.

7. CAD MODELLING
The individual components were modelled using SolidWorks.
The major CAD components include:
•	Sun Gear
•	Planet Gear
•	Ring Gear
•	Planet Carrier
•	Planet Pins
The models were developed with consideration for gear geometry, mating surfaces, shaft/bore interfaces, carrier geometry, and assembly constraints.
Where applicable, lightening holes/cutouts were incorporated into the component geometry to reduce unnecessary mass while retaining the required structural material.

8. PLANET CARRIER DESIGN
The Planet Carrier was designed to support three Planet Gears while maintaining their correct position relative to the Sun Gear.
The Planet Pins are positioned at 120° intervals around the central axis.
The carrier design incorporates:
•	Central rotational interface
•	Three Planet Pin locations
•	Planet Pin support geometry
•	Appropriate clearances
•	Structural web geometry
•	Weight-reduction features where applicable
The carrier was subsequently integrated into the complete planetary gear assembly.

•	Planetary centre distances

•	Planet spacing

13. RESULTS
The final CAD assembly successfully represents a three-planet planetary gear mechanism.
The project demonstrated:
•	Successful generation of compatible gear geometry.
•	Correct geometric arrangement of the Sun, Planet, and Ring gears.
•	Successful assembly of the planetary mechanism.
